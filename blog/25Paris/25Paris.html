Museums and Boulevards

Four days in Paris. The Orsay seen through its own clock, the Winged Victory on the Louvre’s staircase, Versailles from the Hall of Mirrors out to the Grand Canal, two of Notre-Dame’s rose windows, the mural wall behind the Stravinsky fountain…

Trip Notes
Place France · Paris
Landmarks Musée d'Orsay · the Louvre · Versailles · Notre-Dame · Place Igor-Stravinsky · the Arc de Triomphe · the Eiffel Tower · Place de la Concorde · Grande Galerie de l'Évolution
What we saw A clock as big as a wall · a marble figure with no arms on a ship's prow · a hall of mirrors · two rose windows · a wall painted four storeys high · the Arc and the Tower · the river and its bridges · the African caravan under the glass roof
The Winged Victory at the top of the Daru staircase — no head, no arms, and a stone ship’s prow under her feet.
Versailles, the inner courtyard: brick, stone, gold leaf, and a clock in the middle of the attic.
The Hall of Mirrors: every chandelier lit twice.
From the château steps: the parterres, the long green lawn, then the Grand Canal going west until it stops.
Notre-Dame’s north rose — Mary and the Child at the centre, kings and prophets out in the petals.
Notre-Dame’s south rose, the “midday rose”: eighty-four medallions of the Last Judgement, and the only one of the three that gets the noon light.
The wall behind the Stravinsky fountain: Jef Aérosol’s face with a finger to the lips, Shepard Fairey’s blue “the future is unwritten”, and an Invader mosaic on the right.
The Arc at the top of the Champs-Élysées, with the traffic going round it.
The Eiffel Tower.
The Seine at the Concorde: five low arches, the two horses of Marly on their pedestals, and the Sacré-Cœur on its hill.
The last afternoon, in the Jardin des Plantes: the Grande Galerie de l'Évolution — an iron hall, three tiers of balconies, and the African animals queuing across the floor.
