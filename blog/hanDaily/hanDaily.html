Han's Daily Frames 🎬

Han's Daily Frames 🎬 — a personal-IP character, made into daily shorts by an AI pipeline
Bottom line

Han is the 3D Q-version personal IP character I designed. I built an AI workflow on TapNow and collaborate with WorkBuddy and AI agents: it turns a one-line idea — a quiet autumn morning, a climb up Gem Mountain, a tea break — into a finished short with the same character every time. Below are three of those daily films and the process behind them.

00The Films

Three daily shorts, one character

Each film starts from a one-line idea and a shot list, then runs through the workflow. The character setup is done once and reused for every film — so all three share the same soul, Han, yet each can carry a different inspiration, scene, action and mood.

Morning Coffee & Osmanthus Bird. The first coffee of the day, then a small bird settling on the osmanthus outside the window — built shot-by-shot from the storyboard further down.
Gem Mountain: Baoshu & Chenghuang. A character walk-through up Hangzhou's Gem Mountain, framing the pagoda and the old-town geography as a calm, vertical journey.
Catching Inspiration with Tea. A slower, atmospheric piece — a tea break that becomes a small meditation on where ideas come from.

01The Pipeline

TapNow / Tapflow — five modules, cloneable

The whole thing runs as one node graph: character setup → scene concept → visual generation → audio → post & export. The character setup is done once and reused; everything else is fed by a written brief and fill-in prompt templates, so a new film is mostly "swap the idea, keep the rig".

  1. Character setup (once). Han's reference sheets plus a consistency lock pin the look — hat, shirt, shorts, boots and the three front buttons — so the same character holds across every film, front, side and back.
  2. Scene concept. One-line idea → script → shot breakdown (timecode, camera, light, suggested sound). Lock the shots here before generating, to avoid rework.
  3. Visual generation. Text-to-image keyframes per shot, then image-to-video with camera moves; broken frames are fixed with inpainting, not a full re-render.
  4. Audio. Generate BGM and layered SFX, optionally a voiceover, and mix them to the timeline.
  5. Post & export. Edit the shots together, add captions, upscale, export MP4 (H.264).

Prompt templates

The reusable part — fill in, don't rewrite

The pipeline's real asset is the prompt shape. Two templates do most of the work; only the {{placeholders}} change between films.

# text-to-image · keyframe (per shot) 主体 = {{character, front 3 buttons / or flat side-back}} 动作 = {{the shot's action}} 环境 = {{scene / weather / light}} 情绪 = {{mood}} 镜头 = {{shot size + angle}} 风格 = 3D animation film look, soft light 规格 = 9:16 1080x1920 负向 = {{global: back buttons, side roundel, outfit variant, photoreal}} + {{shot-specific}} # image-to-video (per shot) 运镜 = {{push-in / pull-back / pan / tilt-up / orbit / follow}} 动态 = {{camera or subtle character motion}} 时长 = {{seconds per shot}} motion = {{0.25–0.7; low for lyrical, high for action}} seed_lock = on 一致性参考 = {{back shot → back frame; side shot → side frame}}
The negative prompt is the guardrail: it is what keeps Han's back and side frames free of the front-only buttons — the single biggest source of wasted renders.

02Storyboard Example

Morning Coffee & Osmanthus Bird — 5 shots, 10s

I build the shot list together with AI in pre-production. Locking the shots up front is what keeps the generation consistent — the film above came straight from this list.

Shot Time What happens
01 0–2.5s At the bar — starts the espresso machine; the first cup of the day.
02 2.5–4.5s Close-up — coffee pours, steam rises; a relaxed sip.
03 4.5–6.5s Walks to the window with the cup.
04 6.5–8.5s By the window — sips, looks out at the autumn morning.
05 8.5–10s Outside — a small bird lands on the osmanthus; the frame settles.
