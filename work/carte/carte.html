Carte 🎯 - Integrated Product Design of Digital Building Blocks
Bottom line

Building blocks are flat. A child stacks a tower on a table top, and the third dimension goes unpractised. Carte rebuilds the block around a Cartesian core — so every piece must be moved and rotated in X, Y and Z to fit — and then wraps an AI system around it that turns a child's own drawing into a real, printable toy.

3parts of the system — AI model library, game app, printed models
3spatial abilities targeted — XYZ movement, mental rotation, parent co-play
2worlds bridged — virtual practice and physical play
1research question the whole product answers

00Executive Summary

What this project is, and why it works

‘Carte’ is an integrated product design project that helps children develop spatial thinking skills through play. It starts from a recognised problem: children differ in spatial perception, and those differences affect their later learning. The project answers that with an organised product that lets young children cultivate spatial thinking while they are simply playing.

  1. The opportunity was found in what blocks cannot do. Existing block products are excellent, but their play happens largely on a flat plane. Carte makes movement in all three dimensions the point of the toy: pieces only fit when they are rotated and positioned correctly.
  2. Mental rotation is trained deliberately. Because every piece has to be turned to be seated, the child repeatedly observes the same object from different dimensions — the exact practice that builds mental rotation ability.
  3. Parents are designed into the loop, not asked to watch. Parental guidance is what turns knowledge into practice, so co-play is a product requirement rather than a nice-to-have. The whole co-creation model is built around a child and a parent making something together.
  4. Two worlds, one competence. The virtual version lets a child try unlimited models at almost no cost of error; the printed version restores real spatial awareness and hands-on practice. Carte is deliberately built so the same spatial skill transfers between them.

01Design Perspective

Discipline: opportunity framing & product design

The project is built on three opportunities read directly out of how children actually play — and each one becomes a physical property of the product rather than a claim in a slide.

Opportunity 01

Movement in multiple directions

In spatial play the Cartesian coordinate system has always supported good products. Carte takes the 2D plane behaviour of ordinary blocks and extends it deliberately into 3D, so that movement in X, Y and Z is guided by purpose rather than left to chance.

Opportunity 02

Encouraging rotation

By making every piece require turning to fit, the child observes and handles objects from many orientations. That repetition is what deepens their understanding of object structure and improves mental rotation ability with practice.

Opportunity 03

Engaging parents

With parental guidance, children apply their knowledge more effectively. The product is therefore shaped so that parents are more willing to sit down and play alongside their child — a designed outcome, not a hope.

Design intent

The product is not a set of shapes — it is a co-creation system. A child draws something, the drawing is processed into a buildable structure, and the child and parent assemble it together: first in the app, then in their hands. The toy is the thing they made, not the thing they bought.

02Technical Perspective

Discipline: AI pipeline & production

The technical spine of Carte is a three-stage model pipeline that converts a child's drawing into an assemblable, printable object — with the child's own skill level determining how complex that object becomes.

Stage 01

Categorise

When a model is uploaded, the platform inspects it and sorts it by theme and difficulty level. This is what keeps the library age-appropriate without a human editing every entry.

Stage 02

Generate “The Core”

According to that difficulty level, AI generates the model's Core — the basic logical structure that holds the build together and defines where pieces can attach.

Stage 03

Split into modules

The model is divided against a module library and attached to the Core, producing a user library the child selects from. The build is therefore assemblable from standard parts rather than from one bespoke print.

Production

Guided assembly & printing

The app walks the child through assembly with steps locked until the previous one is complete — e.g. body + wing + turret = jet — then splits the model for printing via a home 3D printer through a partnered print-and-delivery service.

Why step-locking matters

Locking each step until the last is finished turns the assembly into a guided sequence rather than a free-for-all, which keeps a 3–5 year old inside their working memory. It also mirrors how the printed pieces physically work — a later piece genuinely cannot attach until the earlier one exists.

03Development & Delivery

Research, product and system
Research & ideation — the three spatial opportunities, moodboard and sketches through to “The Core”, the business model canvas, and the AI back-end that categorises models, generates the Core and splits the build into modules.
Development & delivery — the step-locked app interface and printing flow, the 3D-printed blocks for a second round of physical play, the value framing of virtual versus realistic learning, and the future gesture-recognition and 3D-glasses scenario.
