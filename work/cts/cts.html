Contamination to System 🌊 - An Embodied Interactive Projection with Real-time Generative Visuals
Bottom line

The visitor is not shown pollution — the visitor is the source of it. The moment someone steps into the projection, trash and pollution liquid begin leaking from their hands and body, and the ocean around them mutates in response. Contamination stops being scenery and becomes something the viewer is personally producing.

00Executive Summary

What this project is, and why it works

‘Contamination to System’ explores ocean contamination and the threats to sea creatures caused by humans. The piece takes its premise from a real phenomenon: some sea creatures consume man-made contamination, which then combines inside their bodies and eventually flows back into the sea ecosystem.

  1. Embodiment replaces accusation. A campaign poster tells you that humans pollute. This piece lets you feel it happening through you — the leakage originates at your hands, so there is no distance between the viewer and the cause.
  2. The imagery is generated, not played back. Nothing on the wall is a video file. The visuals are synthesised in real time, which means the ocean's state is a live consequence of the visitor's behaviour rather than a loop that runs regardless.
  3. It comes from a first-hand observation. The concept began with the maker's own experience of diving and sea fishing — watching man-made objects become trash and then be quietly “forgotten” in the water. The work is a documentary impulse, not a slogan.
  4. It ends on a question, not a moral. How do simple contaminations become huge burdens? Where will they go? What will they become? Will they come back to turn on us? The piece withholds its conclusion on purpose.

01Design Perspective

Discipline: concept & interaction design

The design problem is not “how do we visualise ocean pollution” — that is an animation task. It is how do you make an audience feel responsible for something happening at the scale of an ocean, in a room they walked into for a few minutes.

Design decision What it is Why it was made
Make the visitor the emitter Contamination leaks from the visitor's own hands and body on entry. Guilt by proximity is weak; causation experienced first-hand is not. The piece repeatedly returns to the visitor's body as its source.
Start from a real observation Diving and shallow-sea fishing, and watching man-made objects be “forgotten” in the water. It gives the piece documentary grounding — this is not a hypothetical scenario but something the maker watched happen.
Reframe the victim Sea creatures are shown dying from their own original characteristics. Contamination is not an injury inflicted from outside; it is absorbed and then expressed from within. That reframing is the piece's sharpest idea.
End on unanswered questions Where will it go? What will it become? Will it come back to turn on us? The work presents a phenomenon “in an alerting way” but refuses to resolve it. An audience that leaves with a question keeps working on it after the room.
Design intent

Small, ordinary pieces of contamination accumulate into something enormous. The projection is built so that this accumulation is visible while it happens — the visitor watches a trivial act become a systemic one, in real time, inside their own silhouette.

02Technical Perspective

Discipline: real-time generative systems

Technically this is a real-time generative installation, not a played-back film. The visitor's presence is sensed, that data drives a generative model, and the resulting imagery is mapped continuously across the wall and floor around them. Every frame the audience sees is being computed in the moment.

Component 01

Presence sensing

A Zed depth camera reads the visitor's position and silhouette. There is no controller and no instruction — the body is the entire input device, which is what makes the leakage feel attached to the person rather than triggered by them.

Component 02

Real-time generative visuals

Stable Diffusion imagery is driven live through TouchDesigner, so the ocean's condition responds to what the visitor does rather than repeating a fixed sequence. This is the technical core of the piece: generation as behaviour, not as a rendering shortcut.

Component 03

Embodied projection mapping

The output is mapped across two surfaces — wall and floor — so the environment wraps the visitor. Because the mapping is anchored to the room rather than to a screen, the contamination appears to occupy the same space as the body.

Component 04

Asset & scene preparation

Blender handles the modelled and prepared elements that the generative layer composites with, and the audio is assembled from CC0-licensed sound effects — a deliberate choice that keeps the project free of licensing constraints. The plastic-waste models that drift into the ocean began as Stable Diffusion text-to-3D white models (untextured base meshes), then cleaned up and prepared in Blender — an AI step that produced the asset vocabulary far faster than modelling it by hand.

Why real-time matters here

A pre-rendered animation of ocean pollution would be a statement, and the audience would be watching someone else's conclusion. Generating the imagery live makes the state of the ocean an output of the visitor's own presence — which is the only way the piece's argument about accumulation can be experienced instead of merely understood.
